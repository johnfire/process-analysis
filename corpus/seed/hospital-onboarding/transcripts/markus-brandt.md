**Interviewer:** What are you waiting on right now?

**Markus Brandt:** Facilities sign-off for a badge, mostly. And a couple of approved requisitions have been sitting somewhere before they reach our queue. The actual IT bits aren’t the issue.

**Interviewer:** Walk me through the last one of these that went weird. And the one before that.

**Markus Brandt:** Last one was special hardware — somebody decided late they needed a different setup, so the standard bundle was no longer standard. That takes ages because procurement. Before that, the manager wanted access that didn’t fit the usual profile, and we had to sort out what department zone the card should have. Nothing dramatic, just people not deciding things early enough.

**Interviewer:** Who do you have to chase? Who chases you?

**Markus Brandt:** Usually Thomas’s side for the approved requisition, and occasionally facilities for the badge. Petra will chase if her new starter is about to arrive and she hasn’t seen credentials. Jonas gets the workstation-and-badge record through the system, so he sometimes asks where it is.

**Interviewer:** From it landing on you to you being done with it — how much of that is you actually working on it?

**Markus Brandt:** Not long, really. Creating accounts is maybe ten minutes if everything is clean. The hardware order and badge, perhaps half an hour. Of course you have to check the role, put entries into asset inventory, set up the account, set the badge zone, mail the manager, make sure the loaner is usable — so it can take most of an afternoon if you count all the little bits.

**Interviewer:** What comes back to you?

**Markus Brandt:** Very little. Sometimes a request for special hardware, or somebody says the access card has the wrong zone. More often it’s “the manager says they didn’t receive the credentials,” which normally means they’re buried in email.

**Interviewer:** If you were training your replacement and only had a week, what rule would you give them?

**Markus Brandt:** Use the standard profile for the role unless someone gives you a clear reason not to. Don’t invent access packages because a manager says, “They’ll probably need everything.” And get a loaner out if the ordered machine isn’t there.

**Interviewer:** What do you copy from one place into another?

**Markus Brandt:** Name, department, role, start date — from the requisition into AD, the asset system, and badge system. You also end up putting the same person’s details into the email to Petra. It’s not exactly difficult, just tedious.

**Interviewer:** What do you have to reformat before you can use it?

**Markus Brandt:** Requisitions aren’t always consistent. One will have a proper department code, another will say “ward nursing” in a comment field. Then you have to translate that into whatever the badge system calls the zone. The systems don’t speak the same language, obviously.

**Interviewer:** What do you look up or re-explain every single time?

**Markus Brandt:** The profile mapping for roles, mainly. And which zone belongs to which unit. People also ask what happens to credentials, whether the laptop has arrived, whether a loaner is okay — same questions, every time.

**Interviewer:** Whose sign-off do you need? Has that person ever actually sent something back — when?

**Markus Brandt:** I need the approved requisition before I start, from Thomas’s side, and facilities sign-off before badge printing goes through. Yes, things get sent back — usually when the department or role is unclear. It happened recently with a nursing starter whose location changed after the request was approved.

**Interviewer:** What do you keep in your own spreadsheet or notes because the system won’t hold it?

**Markus Brandt:** A simple tracker of who has received a loaner and what is still outstanding. Asset inventory has the equipment, but it’s not good for seeing the whole picture at a glance. There are notes about exceptions too, so we don’t have the same conversation twice.

**Interviewer:** What deadline are you always up against, and what makes it tight?

**Markus Brandt:** Start date. Everyone suddenly remembers it a day or two beforehand, although the hiring process has apparently been going on for weeks. Then Petra needs the person able to work on day one, and we’re expected to make that happen regardless of what arrived late.

**Interviewer:** What’s the worst thing that happened when this went wrong?

**Markus Brandt:** A new starter arrived without the right access and couldn’t get into the relevant area or systems straight away. They had a loaner, I think, but it still made the department look unprepared. That was because the information changed late, not because account creation is some mystery.

**Interviewer:** If you vanished for two weeks with no laptop, what would be broken when you got back?

**Markus Brandt:** The queue would build up, certainly. The service desk can create accounts, but they’d have to ask around about the odd cases and loaner allocations. Badges might wait on facilities anyway. It wouldn’t collapse, but there would be a lot of messages.

**Interviewer:** What do you do that you’re fairly sure is pointless?

**Markus Brandt:** Chasing people for details that ought to be on the requisition in the first place. And sending separate confirmation emails when the system already shows the ticket complete. But if you don’t send them, somebody says they were never told.
