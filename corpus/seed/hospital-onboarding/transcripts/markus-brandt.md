**Markus Brandt:** Not long, really. Accounts go through the service desk’s morning batch, so usually the next morning. Equipment depends on what is already ahead of it. The queue can look busy, but that is procurement and stock, not me sitting on a ticket.

**Interviewer:** What comes back to you?

**Markus Brandt:** Very little. Sometimes a special-hardware request needs sorting out. Otherwise nothing really comes back—once the account and card are done, they’re done. If someone has an issue after that, it tends to be a different ticket.

**Interviewer:** If you were training your replacement and only had a week, what rule would you give them?

**Markus Brandt:** Use the standard role bundle. Create the standard profile set, set the badge to the department’s zone, and send the credentials to the manager. Don’t start improvising because someone says they need an exception without it being properly requested.

**Interviewer:** What do you copy from one place into another?

**Markus Brandt:** The employee and role information goes from the ticket into Active Directory, then badge details into the badge system, and equipment into asset inventory. It’s normal admin work. You have to make sure the names match, because otherwise somebody gets a badge that doesn’t line up with their account and then everybody rings IT.

**Interviewer:** What do you have to reformat before you can use it?

**Markus Brandt:** Nothing dramatic. The request details are not always presented the same way as the fields in AD or the badge system, so you translate them into the standard profile and department zone. That is just how the systems work.

**Interviewer:** What do you look up or re-explain every single time?

**Markus Brandt:** The role profile and which department zone applies, mainly. People often assume “nursing” is enough detail, but it isn’t always. Then you’re checking the request and explaining why we cannot just give broad access because somebody asked in an email.

**Interviewer:** Whose sign-off do you need? Has that person ever actually sent something back — when?

**Interviewer:** What do you keep in your own spreadsheet or notes because the system won't hold it?

**Markus Brandt:** I don’t keep a separate spreadsheet for this. The ticket, asset inventory, and badge system should hold what they need to hold. If people are keeping shadow lists, that is usually because they don’t trust the ticket status.

**Interviewer:** What deadline are you always up against, and what makes it tight?

**Markus Brandt:** The employee’s start date, naturally. It gets tight when approval arrives late or someone adds requirements late. Then suddenly it is IT’s emergency, even though the request has been sitting elsewhere for ages.

**Interviewer:** What's the worst thing that happened when this went wrong?

**Markus Brandt:** The worst practical outcome is someone arrives without the correct account or card and the department manager has to work around it. It creates a lot of noise for what is usually missing input or a late change. We provision what we’re given.

**Interviewer:** If you vanished for two weeks with no laptop, what would be broken when you got back?

**Markus Brandt:** The account tickets would stack up, and equipment requests would wait for the team. The standard process is documented, so it would not be a catastrophe.
